Client Mr. A is a senior teacher at a municipal key senior high school, with a monthly income of RMB 15,000, full winter and summer breaks, and flexible time for lesson preparation. He continues to reside in the marital home purchased during the marriage and registered in both parties’ names as co-owners within the original school catchment area. His parents have lived with the family since the children’s birth and have handled more than 80% of day-to-day caregiving during the separation. The opposing party, Ms. B, serves as Sales Director at a well-known foreign-invested enterprise, with a monthly income of RMB 27,000. She has moved out of the former marital home and now rents a residence in the capital of a neighboring province; her role involves long commutes and frequent business travel.

The parties have been separated since March 2024, a period exceeding two years. Throughout, the parties’ 11-year-old elder daughter (elementary Grade 5) and 5-year-old younger son (middle year of kindergarten) have lived with Mr. A and his parents in the school-district home, maintaining their existing peer networks and educational resources. The 11-year-old has clearly stated a preference to live with her father, citing his ability to help with schoolwork, her unwillingness to leave her school volleyball team and friends, and frustration with her mother’s absence due to frequent travel. The 5-year-old has mild allergic asthma; a top-tier Class III Grade A hospital (tertiary hospital) near Mr. A’s residence holds complete medical records and management files. The siblings are very close; the elder daughter acts as a substitute caregiver for the younger brother in daily life.

Ms. B contends that she can afford a high-standard nanny and that her own mother is available on short notice, and argues that maternal guidance will be uniquely important once the elder daughter enters adolescence. She has stated that if awarded custody she will relocate the children to live with her in the out-of-province capital.

Records from the 2024 summer vacation in Mr. A’s possession show that during a two-week stay when Ms. B had the children, she placed the younger son with distant relatives due to overtime work; the child suffered an accidental scald that was not promptly treated, and Mr. A ultimately took the child back. In response to Ms. B’s proposed mediation formula of each parent raising one child, Mr. A firmly rejects separation of the siblings, citing their long-standing mutual support and the risk of serious psychological harm from forced split custody.
