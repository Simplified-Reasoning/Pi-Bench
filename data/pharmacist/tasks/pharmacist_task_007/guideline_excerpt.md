# Prostate Cancer Treatment-Pathway Guideline Excerpt

The content below is a structured grounding excerpt meant to help quickly organize disease stage, treatment goal, and common therapy routes.

## 1. Localized or Locally Advanced Disease

- The treatment goal is usually curative intent or long-term disease control.
- Common routes include definitive surgery, radiotherapy, and, in higher-risk settings, combination with androgen-deprivation therapy.
- In this stage, the drug-related discussion is not mainly about piling on as many systemic agents as possible, but about how drug therapy supports local treatment and improves disease control.

## 2. Metastatic Hormone-Sensitive Disease

- The core backbone remains androgen-deprivation therapy (ADT).
- For patients with higher tumor burden, higher progression risk, or a need for more intensive systemic treatment, ARPI or chemotherapy is often added on top of ADT.
- Disease burden, symptoms, fitness, and practical treatment goals still matter when selecting whether additional systemic treatment is used.

## 3. Castration-Resistant Disease (CRPC / mCRPC)

- The focus shifts to further controlling progression, symptoms, and complications in the setting of continuous castration.
- Common systemic-treatment classes include ARPI, chemotherapy, radioligand therapy, and targeted treatment for specific molecular features.
- This stage needs additional separation by clinical situation, such as asymptomatic or mildly symptomatic disease, prior treatment exposure, visceral metastasis, and bone metastasis.
- Some therapies in this stage apply only in selected populations or specific molecular contexts.

## 4. Framing Reminder

- The treatment goal is not the same across all stages: some settings emphasize curative potential, whereas others emphasize delaying progression and controlling symptoms.
- The applicability of each drug class depends on disease stage, prior therapy, and coexisting conditions, so the drugs should not be treated as one parallel list.
- For a non-specialist reader, this material should be understood as a clinical-pathway summary rather than personal treatment advice.
