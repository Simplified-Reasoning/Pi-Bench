# Revised TNBC Review Outline (v3 — April 2026)

## Flaws Corrected From the v2 Draft

1. **TROPION-Breast02 population was mislabeled "PD-L1-negative."** The trial enrolled patients "for whom immunotherapy was not an option" — this includes patients with PD-L1-expressing tumors who were ineligible for ICI due to prior neoadjuvant ICI exposure, comorbidities, or geographic inaccessibility (AstraZeneca press release, 19 Oct 2025; BusinessWire, 18 Oct 2025). Calling the population "PD-L1-negative" was inaccurate and has been corrected to "immunotherapy-ineligible (mixed PD-L1 status)."

2. **ASCENT-03 timeline was conflated.** The Gilead press release announcing topline results was 27 May 2025; the full presentation was at ESMO 2025 Congress, 17–21 October 2025, Berlin (Cortés J, et al. LBA20). The v2 draft cited both dates without distinguishing them.

3. **Major omission: DESTINY-Breast04 and the HER2-low/ultralow reclassification.** T-DXd showed PFS 8.5 vs 2.9 mo (HR 0.46) and OS 18.2 vs 8.3 mo (HR 0.48) in the HR-negative (i.e., TNBC) HER2-low subgroup of DESTINY-Breast04 (Modi S, et al. N Engl J Med. 2022;387(1):9-20. PMID: 35665782). DESTINY-Breast06 extended T-DXd to HER2-ultralow (IHC 0 with faint staining), and the FDA approved T-DXd for HER2-low/ultralow in January 2025. This reclassification means a substantial fraction of patients previously classified as "TNBC" (HER2 IHC 0) are now HER2-low or HER2-ultralow and eligible for T-DXd. The v2 draft omitted this entirely — it is a paradigm shift that redefines the TNBC population boundary.

4. **The nanocarrier-to-ADC "bridge" was overclaimed.** The v2 draft stated ADCs are "multivalent targeting systems governed by the same avidity principles" as nanocarriers. This is misleading: IgG antibodies are bivalent (2 antigen-binding sites), not multivalent in the nanocarrier sense (tens to hundreds of ligands per particle). The avidity physics is qualitatively different — nanocarrier multivalency exploits cooperative rebinding across many low-affinity contacts, while ADC bivalency relies on high-affinity monoclonal antibody binding. DAR (drug-to-antibody ratio) governs payload delivery, not targeting valency. The bridge is real but weaker and more nuanced than claimed. Corrected to: "The receptor-density dependence of ADC efficacy echoes nanocarrier selectivity principles, but the valency regimes are different (bivalent vs. multivalent), and no study has tested whether nanocarrier-derived design rules translate to ADC optimization."

5. **TROP-2 as a predictive biomarker was oversimplified.** The ASCENT biomarker analysis (Bardia A, et al. Ann Oncol. 2021;32(9):1148-1156. DOI: 10.1016/j.annonc.2021.07.005) showed SG benefit regardless of TROP-2 expression level, though greater efficacy in medium/high TROP-2 expressors. The NeoSTAR trial found TROP-2 expression was NOT predictive of pCR to neoadjuvant SG (Abelman RO, et al. Ann Oncol. 2024;35:293-301. PMID: 38092228). The v2 draft's claim that "TROP-2 expression level correlates with SG response" was an oversimplification. Corrected to: "TROP-2 expression shows a trend toward greater SG benefit at higher levels in the metastatic setting, but is not a validated predictive biomarker and was non-predictive in the neoadjuvant NeoSTAR trial."

6. **Neoadjuvant ADC frontier was missing.** NeoSTAR (SG ± pembrolizumab, phase 2: SG/P arm pCR 34%, ASCO 2025 Abstract 511) and TROPION-Breast03 (Dato-DXd ± durvalumab for residual disease post-neoadjuvant, phase 3, NCT05629585, ongoing) represent the next battleground beyond metastatic TNBC. The v2 draft covered only metastatic settings.

7. **CSV tier assignment was inconsistent.** "Frontiers Immunol 2024 TME subtyping" was labeled Tier 2 but is a review article. Corrected to Tier 4.

## Revised Storylines (3 main + 1 secondary, ranked by presentability)

### Storyline 1 — The 2025 ADC Revolution and the Three Problems It Created

**Why this is first:** 2025 was the year ADCs moved from second-line salvage to first-line standard-of-care in mTNBC. Three phase III trials reported within months of each other, collectively covering both PD-L1-positive and immunotherapy-ineligible populations. Simultaneously, the HER2-low/ultralow reclassification (DESTINY-Breast04/06) is shrinking the "true TNBC" population. This is the most consequential shift in TNBC treatment since pembrolizumab's approval — and it created three unsolved problems: sequencing, cross-resistance, and population redefinition.

**What has been achieved (2025 data):**

- **ASCENT-04/KEYNOTE-D19 (ASCO 2025):** SG + pembrolizumab vs chemo + pembrolizumab in PD-L1+ (CPS ≥10) first-line mTNBC (n=443). Median PFS 11.2 mo (95% CI 9.3–16.7) vs 7.8 mo (95% CI 7.3–9.3); HR 0.65; 35% risk reduction. Median DOR not yet reached vs 8.3 mo. OS immature. New SOC for PD-L1+ first-line mTNBC. [de Azambuja E, Kalinsky K, Loi S, et al. J Clin Oncol. 2025;43(17_suppl):LBA109. DOI: 10.1200/JCO.2025.43.17_suppl.LBA109.]

- **ASCENT-03 (ESMO 2025, October, Berlin):** SG vs chemo in PD-(L)1-inhibitor-ineligible first-line mTNBC (n=558). Median PFS 9.7 mo (95% CI 8.1–11.1) vs 6.9 mo (95% CI 5.6–8.2); HR 0.62; 38% risk reduction. ORR 48% vs 46% but DOR 12.2 vs 7.2 mo. OS immature; crossover permitted and may complicate OS interpretation. Data cutoff: 2 April 2025. [Cortés J, et al. ESMO 2025, LBA20. Gilead topline press release: 27 May 2025; full presentation: 18 Oct 2025.]

- **TROPION-Breast02 (ESMO 2025, October, Berlin):** Dato-DXd vs investigator's choice of chemotherapy (ICC) in first-line mTNBC for patients for whom immunotherapy was not an option (n=644; includes both PD-L1-negative and PD-L1-positive patients ineligible for ICI due to prior neoadjuvant ICI, comorbidities, or geographic inaccessibility). Median PFS 10.8 vs 5.6 mo (HR 0.57; 95% CI 0.47–0.69; p<0.0001). **Median OS 23.7 vs 18.7 mo (HR 0.79; 95% CI 0.64–0.98; p=0.0291) — first significant OS benefit in immunotherapy-ineligible first-line mTNBC.** ORR 62.5% vs 29.3%; DOR 12.3 vs 7.1 mo. Dato-DXd granted FDA Priority Review (2026). [Dent RA, Shao Z, Schmid P, et al. Ann Oncol. 2025;36(suppl):LBA21. DOI: 10.1016/S0923-7534(25)04801-X. AstraZeneca press release, 19 Oct 2025.]

**What was already established (pre-2025):**

- Pembrolizumab + chemo: FDA-approved for PD-L1 CPS ≥10 mTNBC (KEYNOTE-355 final OS: HR 0.73) and neoadjuvant early TNBC (KEYNOTE-522: pCR 64.8% vs 51.2%, EFS benefit regardless of PD-L1). [Cortes J, et al. N Engl J Med. 2022;387(3):217-226. DOI: 10.1056/NEJMoa2202809. PMID: 35857659.] [Schmid P, et al. N Engl J Med. 2022;386(6):556-567. DOI: 10.1056/NEJMoa2112651. PMID: 35139274.]
- SG monotherapy: FDA-approved for relapsed/refractory mTNBC (ASCENT: PFS 5.6 vs 1.7 mo, OS 12.1 vs 6.7 mo). [Bardia A, et al. N Engl J Med. 2021;384(16):1529-1541. DOI: 10.1056/NEJMoa2028485. PMID: 33882206.]
- T-DXd for HER2-low: FDA-approved (DESTINY-Breast04: all-patient PFS 9.9 vs 5.1 mo, HR 0.50; OS 23.4 vs 16.8 mo, HR 0.64; **HR-negative subgroup: PFS 8.5 vs 2.9 mo, HR 0.46; OS 18.2 vs 8.3 mo, HR 0.48**). [Modi S, et al. N Engl J Med. 2022;387(1):9-20. DOI: 10.1056/NEJMoa2203690. PMID: 35665782.]
- T-DXd for HER2-ultralow: FDA-approved January 2025 (DESTINY-Breast06: HR+/HER2-low and HER2-ultralow; PFS 13.2 vs 8.1 mo, HR 0.63). [Curigliano G, et al. N Engl J Med. 2024. DOI: 10.1056/NEJMoa2407571.]
- Olaparib (OlympiAD/OlympiA) and talazoparib (EMBRACA): FDA-approved for gBRCA-mutated TNBC. [Robson M, et al. N Engl J Med. 2017;377(6):523-533. PMID: 28578601.] [Litton JK, et al. N Engl J Med. 2018;379(8):753-763. PMID: 30110579.] [Tutt ANJ, et al. N Engl J Med. 2021;384(25):2394-2405. PMID: 34081848.]

**Three unsolved problems:**

*Problem 1 — ADC sequencing and cross-resistance:*
- SG and Dato-DXd both target TROP-2 but differ in linker chemistry, payload (SN-38 vs DXd), DAR, and toxicity profile (SG: more neutropenia/diarrhea; Dato-DXd: more stomatitis/dry eye/ocular toxicity, less hematologic toxicity). Direct comparison is impossible from separate trials (different populations, different chemo backbones, different risk profiles).
- Both use topoisomerase-I inhibitor payloads. Whether resistance to one predicts resistance to the other is unknown. Mechanisms include: TROP-2 antigen downregulation/loss, MDR transporter (P-gp/ABCB1) upregulation, topoisomerase-I mutations, and concurrent genomic alterations. [Cancer Drug Resist. 2025. DOI: 10.20517/cdr.2025.148.]
- No prospective sequencing trial exists. The field urgently needs one.

*Problem 2 — Biomarker inadequacy:*
- PD-L1 CPS ≥10 defines pembrolizumab eligibility but was non-predictive in neoadjuvant TNBC (KEYNOTE-522). ~70% of mTNBC patients are immunotherapy-ineligible by this criterion.
- TROP-2 expression shows a trend toward greater SG benefit at higher levels (ASCENT biomarker analysis: Bardia A, et al. Ann Oncol. 2021;32(9):1148-1156. DOI: 10.1016/j.annonc.2021.07.005) but was NOT predictive of pCR in the neoadjuvant NeoSTAR trial (Abelman RO, et al. Ann Oncol. 2024;35:293-301. PMID: 38092228). TROP-2 is not a required companion diagnostic for SG or Dato-DXd.
- TILs, TMB, ctDNA, and Lehmann subtyping (BL1/BL2/M/LAR) are prognostic but not validated for treatment selection. The FUTURE trial (NCT03805399) showed subtype-guided treatment improved PFS in a small phase II, but this is not yet SOC. [Lehmann BD, et al. J Clin Invest. 2011;121(7):2750-2767. PMID: 21633166.] [Lehmann BD, et al. PLoS One. 2016;11(6):e0157368. PMID: 27310713.]
- The IMpassion130/131 paradox (same drug, same population, different chemo backbone → opposite results) remains unexplained and matters for all future ICI + ADC combinations. [Schmid P, et al. N Engl J Med. 2018;379(22):2108-2121. PMID: 30345906.] [Miles D, et al. Ann Oncol. 2021;32(7):846-857. PMID: 34219000.]

*Problem 3 — The shrinking "true TNBC" population:*
- The HER2-low/ultralow reclassification (DESTINY-Breast04/06) means that patients previously classified as HER2 IHC 0 (and therefore "TNBC") are now being re-tested and reclassified. HER2-low (IHC 1+ or IHC 2+/ISH−) accounts for ~50% of previously HER2-negative breast cancers. HER2-ultralow (IHC 0 with faint incomplete staining) accounts for an additional ~25%.
- Dynamic HER2 status: a Nature study (npj Breast Cancer, 2025) showed HER2-low status is dynamic among TNBC patients, with discordance between primary and metastatic biopsies. Repeat biopsies may reclassify patients.
- This means the "TNBC" population eligible for TROP-2 ADCs (but not T-DXd) is shrinking. Future trial designs must account for this overlap.

**Neoadjuvant ADC frontier (new in this revision):**

- **NeoSTAR (phase 2, NCT04230109):** Neoadjuvant SG alone: ~two-thirds responded, 30% achieved pCR without additional chemotherapy. SG + pembrolizumab arm (Arm A2, ASCO 2025 Abstract 511): pCR 34%. Higher Ki-67 and TILs were predictive of pCR; TROP-2 expression was NOT predictive. [Abelman RO, et al. Ann Oncol. 2024;35:293-301. PMID: 38092228.] [Abelman RO, et al. J Clin Oncol. 2025;43(16_suppl):511.]
- **TROPION-Breast03 (phase 3, NCT05629585):** Dato-DXd ± durvalumab vs investigator's choice (capecitabine ± pembrolizumab) in stage I–III TNBC with residual invasive disease after neoadjuvant therapy. Primary endpoint: invasive disease-free survival (iDFS). Ongoing; results expected ~2026–2027. [Bardia A, et al. Ther Adv Med Oncol. 2024;16:17588359241248336. PMID: 38686016.]

**Next-generation pipeline:**

- **Emiltatug ledadotin (Emi-Le, XMT-1660):** B7-H4-directed ADC with non-topoisomerase-I payload (Dolaflexin auristatin). FDA fast-tracked for advanced/metastatic recurrent TNBC (2025). Phase 1 ORR 23% in B7-H4-high TNBC (n=130, heavily pretreated). B7-H4 is highly expressed in TNBC with limited expression in healthy tissue — potentially complementary to TROP-2 ADCs and may bypass topo-I cross-resistance. [NCT05377996. ESMO Breast Cancer 2025, Abstract 298MO.]
- **DB-1305/BNT325:** Next-generation TROP-2 ADC (BioNTech/DualityBio). Phase 1/2 data in pretreated TNBC (n=26, ESMO 2025). Grade ≥3 TRAEs 34.6%; dose discontinuation 3.8%. Early activity signals. [Hamilton E, et al. Ann Oncol. 2025;36:439. Abstract 557P.]

### Storyline 2 — Ligand Density, Multivalent Targeting, and the Cautious Bridge to ADC Design

**Why this is elevated but the bridge is now qualified:**
The nanocarrier literature has produced quantifiable design rules for multivalent targeting that are among the sharpest mechanistic insights in the drug-delivery field. The v2 draft overclaimed the bridge to ADCs by equating nanocarrier multivalency (tens to hundreds of ligands) with ADC bivalency (2 binding sites per IgG). This revision corrects the bridge: the receptor-density dependence of both systems is real, but the valency regimes are qualitatively different. The honest framing is that nanocarrier design rules *suggest* principles for ADC optimization (e.g., receptor-density-guided patient stratification, tunable DAR) but have not been tested in that context.

**What has been achieved (preclinical, quantifiable design rules):**

- **Threshold avidity effect:** Below ~9 ligands per PAMAM dendrimer NP, avidity approaches single-ligand affinity; above threshold, avidity becomes robust to ligand-number changes. [Deci MB, et al. WIREs Nanomed Nanobiotechnol. 2018;10(3):e1511. DOI: 10.1002/wnan.1511. PMID: 29436157. PMCID: PMC6089677.]
- **Receptor-density stratification:** Multivalent NPs with low-affinity ligands achieve much higher coverage on cells with modestly higher receptor densities — enabling selectivity between ErbB2-low, -medium, and -high breast cancer categories. [Wang J, et al. ACS Nano. 2020;14(1):372-383. DOI: 10.1021/acsnano.9b05689. PMID: 31899613.]
- **Non-equilibrium selectivity amplification:** Under flow conditions, multivalent NPs with low-affinity ligands achieve even higher specificity than equilibrium predictions — a design principle for vascular-targeted delivery. [Haun JB, Hammer DA. PNAS. 2013;110(9):3327-3332. DOI: 10.1073/pnas.1006611107. PMCID: PMC3694107.]
- **Ligand density ≠ maximal density:** Reducing ligand density of high-affinity interactions yielded NPs that bound MCF-7/LNCaP/SK-OV-3 but NOT MDA-MB-231 (TNBC) — TNBC's receptor landscape may require different density tuning. [Nano Convergence. 2021;8:28. DOI: 10.1186/s40580-021-00288-1.]
- **Single-molecule quantification:** Force spectroscopy and single-cell receptor counting enable rational design at single-cell/single-molecule level. [Woythe L, et al. ACS Nano. 2022;16(3):3785-3796. DOI: 10.1021/acsnano.1c08277. PMID: 35274534. PMCID: PMC8945370.]

**The qualified ADC bridge (corrected from v2):**

ADCs are bivalent (2 antigen-binding sites per IgG), not multivalent in the nanocarrier sense. The avidity physics differs: nanocarrier multivalency exploits cooperative rebinding across many low-affinity contacts, while ADC bivalency relies on high-affinity monoclonal antibody binding. However, the receptor-density dependence is shared: TROP-2 expression level trends with SG efficacy (ASCENT biomarker analysis), and HER2 expression level defines T-DXd eligibility (HER2-low vs. HER2-ultralow vs. HER2-0). The nanocarrier insight that "selectivity depends on the ratio of ligand density to receptor density, not on either alone" could inform receptor-density-guided patient stratification for ADCs — but this translation has not been attempted. The bridge is a hypothesis, not a validated principle.

**What has NOT been solved:**

- **No clinical translation of any ligand-density-optimized nanocarrier.** Zero threshold-avidity or receptor-density-stratified NPs have entered clinical trials for TNBC.
- **TNBC-specific receptor landscape is undercharacterized.** MDA-MB-231 showed different binding profiles than other cancer lines, but systematic receptor-density mapping across Lehmann TNBC subtypes (BL1/BL2/M/LAR) has not been done.
- **The <5% nanomedicine translation rate** remains a quantifiable barrier. [Zheng C, et al. BioIntegration. 2021;2(2):57-60. DOI: 10.15212/bioi-2021-0016.]
- **The nanocarrier-to-ADC design-rule bridge is a hypothesis, not validated.** No study has systematically applied nanocarrier avidity principles to optimize ADC targeting in TNBC.

### Storyline 3 — The Nanocarrier Translation Gap: Rich Preclinical Work, Zero TNBC-Specific Approvals

**Why this remains a separate storyline:**
Storyline #2 is about a specific mechanistic design rule. This storyline is about the structural reasons why the broader preclinical nanocarrier ecosystem has produced zero TNBC-specific approved therapies — and the uncomfortable comparison with ADCs that succeeded using related (but not identical) targeting principles.

**What has been achieved (preclinical richness):**

- Targeted liposomes for TNBC: dual-targeting systems, macrophage-delivered liposomes, ligand-decorated formulations — extensive preclinical literature. [Hajimolaali M, et al. J Liposome Res. 2024;34(4):671-696. DOI: 10.1080/08982104.2024.2325963. PMID: 38520185.]
- TME-responsive NPs: pH/enzyme/redox-responsive systems, charge-reversal NPs, biomimetic membrane-coated NPs — conceptually rich, all preclinical. [Cao Y, et al. WIREs Nanomed Nanobiotechnol. 2024;16(2):e1950. DOI: 10.1002/wnan.1950. PMID: 38528388.]
- CSC-targeting nanocarriers: CD44+/CD24−/ALDH1+ markers are prognostic; EGCG-NPs, sulforaphane liposomes, PLGA-FA systems target CSCs preclinically. [Discover Nano. 2024;19:399. DOI: 10.1186/s11671-024-03985-y.]
- Doxil/Caelyx (liposomal doxorubicin): approved for breast cancer generally, not TNBC-specific; no biomarker-guided selection.
- LNP-based mRNA delivery to TNBC PDX tumors: demonstrates feasibility but also preclinical-to-clinical inconsistency. [ACS Pharmacol Transl Sci. 2024. DOI: 10.1021/acsptsci.4c00597.]
- Lipid nanoparticle translational review: numerous investigational liposomal agents in preclinical/clinical development targeting TNBC. [Pharmaceutics. 2025;17(10):1315. DOI: 10.3390/pharmaceutics17101315.]

**What has NOT been solved (the translation gap):**

- **Zero TNBC-specific nanocarrier therapies are FDA-approved.** EndoTAG-1 (paclitaxel in cationic liposomes) is the only TNBC-specific liposome trial with published phase II results — not approved. [Awada A, et al. Ann Oncol. 2014;25(4):824-831. DOI: 10.1093/annonc/mdu018. PMID: 24667715.]
- **Preclinical-to-clinical inconsistency:** Cell line models (MDA-MB-231) do not accurately represent patient tumors; PDX models show different NP uptake profiles. [ACS Pharmacol Transl Sci. 2024. DOI: 10.1021/acsptsci.4c00597.]
- **Regulatory and manufacturing barriers:** <5% of nanoscale therapeutics entering phase I obtain approval. Batch-to-batch reproducibility, characterization standards, and toxicology requirements are stricter than for small molecules. [Zheng C, et al. BioIntegration. 2021;2(2):57-60. DOI: 10.15212/bioi-2021-0016.] [ACS Appl Mater Interfaces. 2025;17(26):37383-37403. DOI: 10.1021/acsami.5c07366.]
- **The central tension:** ADCs have succeeded where nanocarriers have not — both are targeted drug-delivery systems, but ADCs benefit from antibody-engineering infrastructure, established manufacturing, and clear regulatory pathways. The nanocarrier field must either adopt ADC-like development discipline or find niches (e.g., mRNA delivery, combination payloads, TME reprogramming) where ADCs cannot compete.

### Storyline 4 (Secondary) — Cancer Stem Cells & TME: Prognostically Relevant, Therapeutically Immature

**Why deprioritized:**
- CSCs: CD44+/CD24−/ALDH1+ phenotype is prognostic in TNBC but no approved CSC-targeted therapy exists. [Cancers. 2022;14(16):3932. DOI: 10.3390/cancers14163932. PMCID: PMC9476834.]
- TME: immunosuppressive microenvironment limits ICI response (storyline #1) and is a design target for nanocarriers (storyline #3). TME is a context that appears in other storylines, not a standalone therapeutic modality. [Frontiers Immunol. 2024;15:1441667. PMCID: PMC11487198.]
- These areas are worth tracking but should not receive equal presentation weight.

## Open Questions & Controversies Worth Tracking

1. **ADC sequencing and cross-resistance in TNBC.** With SG and Dato-DXd both entering first-line, the optimal sequence (and whether resistance to one predicts failure of the other) is the most urgent clinical question. The shared topoisomerase-I payload class raises cross-resistance concerns. Next-gen ADCs with non-topo-I payloads (e.g., Emi-Le's Dolaflexin auristatin) or novel targets (B7-H4) may offer escape routes. No prospective sequencing trial exists.

2. **Is PD-L1 CPS the right biomarker for ICI in TNBC?** KEYNOTE-522 found PD-L1 non-predictive in neoadjuvant TNBC. The FDA-mandated CPS ≥10 threshold may exclude patients who would benefit. TROP-2 expression is not predictive of neoadjuvant pCR (NeoSTAR). The field needs a composite biomarker or multi-omic classifier.

3. **How will the HER2-low/ultralow reclassification reshape TNBC trial design?** As more patients are reclassified from HER2 IHC 0 to HER2-ultralow, the "true TNBC" population shrinks. Future TROP-2 ADC trials must account for T-DXd eligibility overlap. Dynamic HER2 status (discordance between primary and metastatic biopsies) adds further complexity.

## Deprioritized / Split-Off Areas (explicit)

1. **"Anything involving biomarkers or combination therapy"** as a standalone section: too vague. Biomarkers are cross-cutting annotations; combination therapy is the mechanism behind storylines #1 and #3.

2. **Cancer stem cells as an independent storyline:** prognostically relevant but therapeutically immature. No approved CSC-targeted therapy. Folded into secondary storyline #4.

3. **TME as an independent storyline:** TME is a context, not a modality. It appears as a resistance mechanism in storyline #1 and a design target in storyline #3.

4. **Targeted liposomes / nanoparticle delivery as a broad generic bucket:** this label obscures the sharper design rule inside it. Ligand-density/multivalency elevated to storyline #2; remaining preclinical work in storyline #3.

5. **PARPi as a major growth area:** PARPi benefit is confined to ~5–10% of TNBC (gBRCA-mutated). Expansion to somatic BRCA or HRD-positive TNBC is unresolved. PARPi resistance (reversion mutations, drug efflux) is emerging. Important but not the leading edge of the field in 2025–2026 — ADCs are.

## Revised Ordering Logic

1. **Storyline #1 first** — grounds the review in the 2025 ADC revolution (three practice-changing phase III trials), the HER2-low/ultralow reclassification, and the three problems they created (sequencing, biomarker inadequacy, population redefinition).
2. **Storyline #2 second** — elevates the sharpest mechanistic insight (ligand-density design rules) with a now-qualified bridge to ADC design — honest about what the bridge can and cannot claim.
3. **Storyline #3 third** — the translation gap provides narrative tension: rich preclinical work, zero clinical approvals, and the uncomfortable comparison with ADCs.
4. **Storyline #4 secondary** — CSCs and TME are worth tracking but should not compete for equal attention.

## Evidence Stratification

- **Tier 1 (FDA-approved, phase III):** pembrolizumab + chemo (KEYNOTE-355/522), SG (ASCENT), T-DXd (DESTINY-Breast04/06), olaparib (OlympiAD/OlympiA), talazoparib (EMBRACA).
- **Tier 2 (phase III, practice-changing 2025):** SG + pembrolizumab (ASCENT-04/KEYNOTE-D19), Dato-DXd (TROPION-Breast02), SG monotherapy first-line (ASCENT-03).
- **Tier 3 (phase I/II/III ongoing, pipeline):** Emi-Le / B7-H4 ADC (NCT05377996), DB-1305/BNT325 (NCT05438329), NeoSTAR (NCT04230109), TROPION-Breast03 (NCT05629585), EndoTAG-1, MEDIOLA, PARPi + ICI combinations.
- **Tier 4 (preclinical, quantifiable design rules):** ligand-density threshold avidity, receptor-density stratification, non-equilibrium selectivity amplification, TROP-2 biomarker analyses, TME subtyping reviews.
- **Tier 5 (preclinical, concept-stage):** targeted liposomes for TNBC, TME-responsive NPs, CSC-targeting nanocarriers, biomimetic membrane-coated NPs.
